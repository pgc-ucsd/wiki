# Payroll and Taxes

## Your pay

As a PhD student, you make your money either by teaching (paid for by the department) or doing research (paid for by your advisor). As you are nominally a student, you are paid at a fraction of the full rate (almost always 50%, though 25% is technically the minimum required to retain health insurance and other benefits). As of the most recent negotiations between UAW and the University of California in 2025, these are the wage steps for both TAs and GSRs before tax.

### Teaching Assistant (TA)
*Step placement is based on teaching experience. All incoming students start at Step 1. When you attain three quarters of experience you move to Step 2, after six Step 3.*

| Step | Monthly Rate (100% FTE) | Monthly Take-Home (Standard 50% FTE) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Step 1** | $8,200 | **$4,100** |
| **Step 2** | $8,486 | **$4,243** |
| **Step 3** | $8,740 | **$4,370** |

### Graduate Student Researcher (GSR)
*Step placement is determined pre/post-advancement. Physics sits at steps 3 and 4.*

| Step | Monthly Rate (100% FTE) | Monthly Take-Home (Standard 50% FTE) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Step 1** | $6,348 | **$3,174** |
| **Step 2** | $6,714 | **$3,357** |
| **Step 3** | $7,226 | **$3,613** |
| **Step 4** | $7,776 | **$3,888** |
| **Step 5** | $8,370 | **$4,185** |
| **Step 6** | $8,960 | **$4,480** |

Federal and state tax, as well as FICA deducts between 15-25% from this number. See the later tax section for more information on filing taxes.

!!! danger "The September-to-November gap"
    Your first fall paycheck arrives **November 1**, but you arrive in
    September. You need to cover roughly six weeks of rent, deposits, and
    living costs before any money appears. This is the single most common
    financial surprise for first-years.

## Payroll

Getting paid, on time, is mostly a matter of doing three pieces of setup early.

### Direct deposit — enroll in all three

There are **two campus offices** handling direct deposit, and domestic students
should sign up for all three streams below. You need **Single Sign-On** for
each.

#### 1. Stipend payments — Triton Student System (TSS)

Use the **TritonLink Direct Deposit tool**. **Enroll as soon as possible.**

#### 2 and 3. Payroll and disbursements — UCPath

Covers **payroll** (TA and GSR employment checks) and **disbursements** (travel
and other personal reimbursement). **Enroll before winter quarter employment.**

1. Log in to **UCPath online**.
2. Select **Employee Actions → Income and Taxes → Direct Deposit**.
3. Add, update, or delete your direct deposit information.
4. To add: enter the **bank routing number**, **account type**, **account
   number**, and the deposit amount or percentage.
5. Select **Save**.

!!! warning "Allow two weeks"
    Initial setup can take **up to two weeks** to take effect. Enrolling the
    week before you need money will not work.

UCPath publishes guided simulations for adding, changing, and deleting direct
deposit — ask Physics Payroll (`hr-ap-phys@ucsd.edu`) if you cannot find them.

If you do not enrol, your check is **mailed to the home address on your
employment paperwork** — which is why keeping your address current on
TritonLink matters.

## Employment paperwork

You must complete an **I-9** verifying identity and eligibility to work. Bring
original documents to orientation: either **one document from List A**, or
**one from List B plus one from List C**.

| List | Establishes | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **A** | Both identity **and** work eligibility | US passport (unexpired or expired) · Permanent Resident Card (I-551) · Certificate of US Citizenship (N-560/N-561) · Certificate of Naturalization (N-550/N-570) · Unexpired foreign passport with I-551 stamp or I-94 showing employment authorization · Employment Authorization Card (I-688A) · Unexpired Employment Authorization Document with photograph (I-688B) |
| **B** | Identity only | Driver's license or state ID with photograph · Federal, state, or local government ID with photograph · School ID with photograph · Voter's registration card · US military card or draft record · Military dependent's ID |
| **C** | Work eligibility only | **Social Security card** (unless marked not valid for employment) · Certification of Birth Abroad (FS-545 / DS-1350) · Original or certified birth certificate with official seal · Native American tribal document · US Citizen ID Card (I-197) · Unexpired DHS employment authorization document |

!!! tip "The common combination"
    Most domestic students without a passport bring a **driver's license (List
    B) plus a Social Security card (List C)**. Bring originals — copies are not
    accepted.

## Ecotime

**Ecotime** is the campus timekeeping system, integrated with UCPath for pay
and leave reporting.

Since October 2023, all **UAW-represented employees** — GSRs, ASEs, postdocs,
and some academic researchers — **must report time in Ecotime**.

!!! warning "Submit a timecard every pay period"
    You must "Complete" (submit for approval) a timecard **each pay period as
    verification of time worked**, even when you have no leave, PTO, or unpaid
    time to report. A blank period still needs a submitted timecard.

Supervisors and delegates approve timecards on the established schedule.
Guidance and office hours are on the Ecotime Blink page. An email is sent out with a reminder to submit the timesheet around the 7th of each month from the HR department.

## Social Security Number

If you are authorized for paid employment, your employer will request your SSN.

International students need an **authorization letter from the International
Services and Engagement Office (ISEO)** before applying. The full process is on
the
[ISEO Social Security Number page](https://ispo.ucsd.edu/advising/financial/social-security-number.html).

Start this early — it gates your ability to be paid.

## Taxes

Salaried students (TAs and GSRs) have their taxes automatically withheld in their monthly paychecks. However, for students on fellowship stipends, tax is not automatically withheld despite fellowship funds being fully taxable. Instead, ***they are expected to file quarterly tax payments to avoid penalties when filling out tax returns***. For more information on filling out tax payments on fellowship stipends, follow [this link](https://traininggrants.ucsd.edu/trainees/taxinformation.html).

### Filing your returns

Salaried students filing tax returns will receive a W-2 electronically via UCPath, as well as a 1098-T, obtained from [tsc1099.com](https://www.tsc1099.com) (you will be provided login information when the form becomes available). Students entirely funded on fellowships will not receive a standard W-2, and instead stipends are broken down on the 1098-T. For students who are partially funded by scholarships and partially by the department or their advisor, they will receive a W-2 for those funds.

**Tax returns are due April 15 of the following tax year.** You may receive some money back or be required to pay additional tax.

### International students

International students face additional compliance requirements to ensure their payments are taxed and reported correctly under U.S. law. **Any international visitor receiving payment or benefits from UCSD must complete a GLACIER record**. The only exceptions are permanent residents, asylum-seekers, or those on DACA status. The GLACIER system collects tax-related information from foreign visitors to help the university determine tax residency, tax withholding rates, and income tax treaty eligibility. International students expecting a stipend payment must complete a GLACIER record to determine appropriate taxation.

UCSD generally provides access to [GLACIER Tax Prep](http://blink.ucsd.edu/go/glacier) (GTP) free of charge to nonresidents affiliated with the university. GTP is an online system that helps prepare nonresident federal tax returns, such as Form 1040NR or Form 1040NREZ. Instead of, or in addition to, other tax documents, international students will typically receive a Form 1042-S detailing their income and any withheld taxes. This form is usually available online through GLACIER by mid-March of the following tax year.

See [here](https://blink.ucsd.edu/sponsor/hr/divisions-units/data/hr-payroll/foreign/returns.html) for more info.

### Tax Return Services & Filing Guidelines

#### Commercial Tax Software (TurboTax, H&R Block, TaxAct)
These are best for domestic students with standard W-2 employment income. While heavily advertised as "free," entering fellowship stipends (1098-T) typically forces an upgrade to a paid tier.

!!! danger "Crucial warning for international students"
    International students **must never** use standard commercial software. It will erroneously file standard resident forms (Form 1040) instead of nonresident forms, which violates U.S. tax law and can jeopardize future visa or green card applications.

### Free Alternatives (Domestic Students)

*   **IRS Free File:** Provides completely free federal filing for individuals with an Adjusted Gross Income (AGI) of $89,000 or less (for the 2026 tax season/2025 tax year).
*   **VITA (Volunteer Income Tax Assistance):** IRS-sponsored free tax preparation for qualifying individuals. UCSD regularly partners with local VITA clinics.
*   **IRS Free File Fillable Forms:** Free electronic versions of paper tax forms for those earning above the AGI threshold, though they lack guided Q&A interfaces.

### International Student Filing Stipulations

International students (F-1 and J-1 visas) are generally classified as "Nonresident Aliens" for their initial years in the U.S. and must adhere to specific rules:

- **Mandatory Form 8843:** Must be filed annually by every international student and their dependents, even if they earned $0 in U.S. income.
- **Form 1040-NR:** Required to report any U.S.-sourced income (e.g., TA/GSR salary, internships, or taxable fellowships).
- **Approved Software:** International students should use **GLACIER Tax Prep (GTP)**, which is generally provided free of charge by UCSD. Alternatively, **Sprintax** is a commercial software specifically designed to navigate nonresident tax treaties, calculate the Substantial Presence Test, and generate Forms 1040-NR and 8843.
