# Research

Finding a group, the deadlines attached to that, and the mechanics of doing
research here.

!!! warning "The deadline that matters"
    You must **secure a research advisor by the end of spring quarter of your
    second year.** Everything else on this page works backward from that.

## Finding a group

The department front-loads the introductions:

- **PHYS 261 — Physics Research at UC San Diego** runs weekly in **winter
  quarter**, specifically to introduce you to the department's research and the
  faculty doing it. It is scheduled not to conflict with graduate courses.
  Register for 0 or 1 unit. This is the single most efficient way to survey the
  department.
- **PHYS 260 — Colloquium**, Thursdays at 4:00 p.m., all year.
- **Area seminars** (for example PHYS 251, High Energy Seminar) — register for
  0 or 1 unit if you will attend regularly.
- **Tea time**, weekly during the academic year, is the informal version. See
  [Student Life](../student-life/index.md#community).

**Start research in the summer after your first year.** That is the expected
point, and summer research assistantships are often available then, subject to
federal funding. Arrange these directly with the professor running the project.

## Funding follows the advisor

After the first year, students are encouraged to affiliate with a research
group and be supported as a **Graduate Student Researcher (GSR)** rather than a
TA. Ask the faculty member you want to work with about support **well in
advance** — spring quarter for the following year.

GSR funding comes from federal contracts and grants, so appointments depend on
funds being available that year. Some TA positions remain available to advanced
students, and fellowship holders can often hold a partial TA or GSR appointment
alongside. See [Funding and Fees](../resources/funding.md).

## Registering for research units

| Course | Who |
| --- | --- |
| **PHYS 298** | Students **not yet** advanced to candidacy |
| **PHYS 299** | Students **advanced** to candidacy |

Both are S/U graded. Research units are how most students reach the 12-unit
minimum once coursework thins out.

## Advancing to candidacy

To advance you need the departmental requirements met **and** a faculty
research supervisor. A doctoral committee is appointed, conducts your
**qualifying examination** — usually a presentation of your thesis research
plan — and on success you advance and receive the **C.Phil. degree**.

**Target: advance by the end of your third year.** Full detail in
[PhD Requirements](../coursework/phd-requirements.md#qualifying-exam-and-advancement-to-candidacy).

## Year-by-year

| Year | What is expected |
| --- | --- |
| **First** | Coursework. Begin research over the summer; the Qualification Committee reviews your first-year grades. |
| **Second** | Finish core courses and electives. Begin advanced courses. **Secure a research advisor by end of spring.** |
| **Third** | Research. Finish course requirements. **Advance to candidacy.** |
| **Fourth+** | Research, write the thesis, defend before your PhD committee. |

## Every year, regardless

A written progress report is due to your advisor by **June 30**, and the
department submits an evaluation of you to GEPA each spring. Continued funding
depends on it — see [Annual Evaluation](annual-evaluation.md).
