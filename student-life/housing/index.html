    Housing¶

    On-campus housing from HDH¶

    UCSD offers housing on-campus through their Graduate and Family Housing program. These comprise modern apartment-style housing units, ranging from studios to multiple bedrooms. Units designed for students with children are also provided.

    You can apply for housing here using your active directory credentials.

    Apply early

    The campus housing queue is long. Get in it as soon as you accept your offer — you can always decline later.

    Waitlists for the different communities are listed here. In your application you are permitted to select multiple preferences, it is advised you select at least one that has a wait time shorter than the time between the application submission and your estimated move-in date. You should aim to move in before orientation.

    Generally speaking, UCSD guarantees on-campus housing for two years, at which point they will likely not renew your rental agreement (although you are free to try). This is two years after you moved in, so if you move July of 2026, you will be kicked out July of 2028. You should begin looking for off-campus housing at least as early as Spring quarter the year before your two-year period ends.

    Off-campus housing¶

    A variety of rooms and apartments exist within a commutable distance from campus. UCSD provides some resources to help find off-campus housing, but generally I've (Matt) found the best resources for finding housing, either with house/roommates or for entire units, has either been word-of-mouth within the department or UCSD network, or to use Facebook Marketplace and/or housing pages.

    Warning about online forums like Facebook

    These forums tend to be overrun with scammers and bots. Search at your own risk, exercise discretion, and never under any circumstances send anyone money (application fees, deposit, etc.) before seeing the unit in-person.

    If you're looking to rent an entire unit, you can search on places such as Zillow or Apartments.com. A helpful bit of advice I've (Matt) found for this, is to look for available units in your price range, then call the listers. Usually these are leasing agencies who may have more units available, or units that will soon become available and are not listed yet.

    Off-campus communities¶

    Some options for living off-campus are:

    • Immediately off-campus: There are a number of apartments in the rectangle between the 5 and 805, and Rose Canyon and campus. These are generally the most expensive rental options, but you are conveniently 1-2 trolley stops from campus or within biking distance. You are also close to UTC, and the cluster of stores by Nobel Drive. Lots of undergrads live here.
    • North Park: Residential neighborhood between the university and downtown San Diego. Rent is generally on the lower end of SD prices (depending on location within the neighborhood). Commutable via car (15-45 minutes depending on traffic), or via trolley blue line and bus (~1 hour). Lots of cafes and restaurants.
    • Pacific Beach: Lively area conveniently located on a blue line trolley stop, making for a 20 minute commute. Rent is on the pricier side. Quite a few bars, popular location for people at a night out.
    • Mira Mesa: Located east and slightly north of campus, by the MCAS Miramar. Rent can range, but is cheaper than what you'll get immediately off-campus. The 31 bus follows Miramar and can get you to to around UTC, which you can transfer onto a different line to get to campus, in around 30-45 minutes depending on traffic. Lots of breweries and Indian food in this area. The major trade-off is the jet fighters taking off and landing at the base are loud as hell.
    • Clairemont/Kearny Mesa: West side of 805 is Clairemont, east side is Kearny Mesa. Clairemont is a residential neighborhood with reasonable rent prices, located in proximity to a big plaza on Balboa with tons of stores. The 41 bus takes you directly from a stop on Genessee all the way to Mayer Hall. Across the 805 in Kearny Mesa is the Convoy Asian District, which has a multitude of Asian restaurants and grocery stores (including an H-Mart and two 99 Ranches).
    • Mission Valley: Just north of North Park, off of the 8. Most apartments here are relatively new and as such rent may be higher. Located near the Fashion Valley and Mission Valley Malls, as well as a Costco and Ikea. Can get to campus in around an hour by taking the green line west, transferring at Old Town, and taking the blue north.
    • Even farther options: Most of these options are less than 30 minutes away by car, and an hour away by public transit. There are some options farther away, such as La Mesa, El Cajon, Chula Vista, etc., which are cheaper in rent than those closer to campus, but you will spend more on gas and take longer to get into campus. If you always work from home and your advisor is fine with this, maybe it isn't an issue for you.

    General rent prices¶

    As mentioned earlier, rent prices are highly variable depending on your location. General advice says to pay no more than 30% of your income to housing. This equates to somewhere between $800 and $1,000 (this depends on, if you're TAing, how much experience you accrue, or if you are GSR, whether you advanced or not). This is impractical for housing prices in San Diego on a PhD student's budget, unless you share a room. I'd (Matt) advise you just create a budget, and figure out what a "comfortable" rent range would be; if you spend less on certain things (like gas, eating out, etc.), a higher percentage of your income going to rent might be feasible.

    In general though, this is what I have found to be the general rent prices for certain living configurations. This is per bedroom (so halved if you share a room), per month, median price extended up to ~15 miles from campus, per Zumper as of 2026. Highly variable depending on quality of apartment complex, and expect it to be higher the closer to campus you are.
